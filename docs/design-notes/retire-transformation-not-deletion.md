# A lane's completion is a transformation, not a deletion (prd10 rulings 13–16)

`packages/web/src/scene/retire.ts`

## History

This file used to hold the cord-cut. prd5 ruling 3 had a landed lane disconnect
from the mass and settle into a desaturated scar near the rim; prd10 ruling 2
then had the freed cord compost into motes until no geometry was left at all.
Ruling 13 knowingly took back both halves: *a rhizomorph is a root-cord
network, and a mycelial network does not delete the cords that carried its
nutrients — it keeps them.* Accumulated strands are the picture of growth the
north star asks for; an emptying field is the opposite of what this organism
is.

So completion became a transformation (ruling 15): the return beat survives
exactly as it was built — matter lifts off the cord and streams home
(`motes.ts`), the parcel runs down the strand, the heart takes its permanent
growth ring (`marks/root.ts`) — and at the end the strand *remains*, thinned
and stilled, threaded into the mass exactly where it always was. What the
motes carry home is the lane's vitality, not its existence.

## Why one channel per stage

Heer & Robertson measured staged transitions beating single-shot ones at
exactly this job, and recommend about a second per stage — hence the four
stages in `RETURN` (~1.4s total). What changed with ruling 15 is only the
sentence the stages spell: it used to be "let go — sprang back — went cold",
and it is now "let go — sent its vitality home — went quiet, and stayed."

## Where the timing constants come from

- `TENSION_MS = 150`: Carbon's `moderate-01`, the shortest span that still
  reads as a change rather than a jump.
- `STILL_MS = 450`: Material M3's `long1` — long enough to watch a thinning
  and cooling, short enough the eye isn't bored before it's released.

## `PERSIST_WIDTH_SCALE`'s governance

0.42 is a *starting* number for the density judgement bounded by ruling 14's
safety margins (see the inline comment for the arithmetic), not a settled one
— ruling 16 says a field that stops reading gets more hierarchy, and this is
the first dial to turn. The operator's eye on a thirty-lane replay is the gate
(ruling 8).
